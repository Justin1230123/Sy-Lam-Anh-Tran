Retail Store Operations & ERP Transformation

End-to-end Business Analysis case study

Business analysis, process improvement, requirements, ERP (Odoo)

Challenge. A fashion retailer had fragmented inventory, sales, CRM and purchasing. Store staff could not see stock at other stores, and allocation and replenishment relied on manual work and judgement.

Solution. Odoo was evaluated as one integrated workflow across inventory, sales, CRM, purchasing and stock transfer.

Expected impact. Better inventory visibility, demand-driven allocation, cross-store transfers, stronger CRM follow-up and less dependence on manual processes.

Elicitation methods

Semi-structured interviews with store managers, supervisors and staff. Role-play and observation of how staff search for stock. Artefact review of Excel inventory files, order emails and paper purchase forms.

Root causes

  • Process Allocation driven by Head Office priorities, little post-sale follow-up.
  • Technology Manual tracking with tags and Excel, limited integration with Head Office CRM.
  • Data Purchasing relies on judgement rather than sales and inventory data.
  • People Knowledge concentrated in key staff, high casual turnover.
  • Structure No single system connecting inventory, sales, CRM and purchasing.

Business requirements

  • BR-01 Real-time inventory visibility across stores and warehouses.
  • BR-02 Store demand visibility to support allocation decisions.
  • BR-03 Search, reserve and transfer stock between stores.
  • BR-04 Customer purchase history for personalised recommendations.
  • BR-05 CRM pipeline for follow-up and post-sale engagement.
  • BR-06 Replenishment based on sales history and inventory levels.
  • BR-07 Standardised workflows that reduce dependence on key staff.

Sample user story

As a sales associate, I want to search for products across stores, so that I can help customers get unavailable sizes or styles.

Given the product is unavailable in the current store, when the employee searches the network, then stock at other locations is shown and the employee can start a reservation or transfer request.

Odoo fit-gap

Each requirement was checked for what standard configuration can support and where process or system change is needed. Areas covered: warehouses per store, reordering rules, CRM pipeline, purchase approval and stock transfers.

FreshMart Automated Inventory Management System

Business Analysis case study (COIT20248, hypothetical scenario)

Root cause analysis, requirements, process modelling, business case

Challenge. Five stores managed inventory with spreadsheets and paper records, causing inaccurate stock, expired perishables, late reordering and no cross-store visibility.

Key finding. The core problem was a lack of accurate, centralised and timely inventory information, not outdated software. Stockouts and wastage were downstream effects.

Proposed solution. A web-based inventory system on a central database with low-stock and expiry alerts, a management dashboard and online stock visibility for customers.

View case study

AS-IS bottlenecks

Manual data entry, separate store records, delayed stock updates and no standard reorder process.

From current state to business need

Current stateGapBusiness need
Manual stock entryHigh risk of human errorAutomated inventory updates
Spreadsheet per storeNo central visibilityCentralised database
Manual expiry checksPerishables may expire unnoticedExpiration tracking
Manual low-stock checksLate reorders and stockoutsReorder alerts

Business requirements

  • BR-01 Improve inventory accuracy by reducing manual entry.
  • BR-02 Up-to-date inventory information across all stores.
  • BR-03 Track expiry and flag products approaching expiry.
  • BR-04 Identify low stock and start replenishment on time.
  • BR-05 Customers can see stock availability online.

Sample user story

As a store manager, I want an alert when stock falls below the reorder level, so that I can reorder before a stockout occurs.

Given stock falls below the reorder level, when the inventory is updated, then the system raises a low-stock alert and the manager can view the affected product.

Business case

The CEO's constraints were a development budget up to $1.2M, payback within 2 years and NPV of at least $0.4M. Operating-cost reduction of 15% on $1.55M gives $232,500 a year, or $208,500 after $24,000 maintenance. The 25% reduction in inventory discrepancy was not given a dollar value because the case supplies no baseline.

Risks

Data migration from spreadsheets, user resistance, internet dependency, incorrect data during transition and integration across five stores.
