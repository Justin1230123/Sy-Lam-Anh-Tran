Retail Store Operations and ERP Transformation

End-to-end business analysis case study

Business challenge

A fashion retailer ran inventory, sales, CRM and purchasing as separate processes. Store staff couldn't see stock at other stores, and stock allocation and replenishment depended on manual work and judgement.

Scope: an academic case study based on real retail pain points. Company details are anonymised, and the outcomes below are proposed, not measured.

Stakeholder analysis (7 groups)

StakeholderMain need or pain point
Head Office, MerchandisingLimited visibility of store-level stock
Head Office, CRM and MarketingFragmented customer data
Store managersManual stock management
Sales associatesLimited CRM and cross-store stock visibility
CustomersDifficulty finding preferred sizes and styles
ProcurementReplenishment based on judgement
IT and systemsFragmented tools and systems

How I gathered requirements

  • Semi-structured interviews with store managers, supervisors and staff to find operational inefficiencies.
  • Role-play and observation: acting as a customer to see how staff search for stock and handle unavailable items.
  • Artefact review of Excel inventory files, order emails and paper purchase forms.

As-is findings and root causes

  • Inventory: manual counting and Excel records, error-prone and with no real-time view.
  • Allocation: Head Office prioritised new collections over local demand.
  • Customer service and CRM: staff saw only their own store's stock, and customer data sat at Head Office, so cross-store sales and follow-ups were missed.
  • Replenishment: purchase requests relied on judgement, not sales and inventory data.
  • People and structure: process and Excel knowledge sat with a few key staff, casual turnover was high, and no single system connected inventory, sales, CRM and purchasing.

Gap analysis

AreaAs-isTo-beGap
InventoryManual Excel trackingReal-time visibilityNo integrated real-time system
AllocationNew collections firstDemand-drivenLimited real-time demand data
Stock visibilityOwn store onlyNetwork-wideNo store-to-store view
CRMMainly Head OfficeStore-level CRMLimited synchronisation

Business requirements

  • BR-01 Real-time inventory visibility across stores and warehouses.
  • BR-02 Visibility of store demand to support allocation decisions.
  • BR-03 Search, reserve and transfer stock between stores.
  • BR-04 Customer purchase history for personalised recommendations.
  • BR-05 CRM pipeline for follow-up and post-sale engagement.
  • BR-06 Replenishment based on sales history and inventory levels.
  • BR-07 Standardised workflows that reduce dependence on key staff.

Traceability example: BR-03 became FR-03, "the system should let sales staff search products across stores and start a reservation or stock transfer."

Example user story

As a sales associate, I want to search for products across stores, so that I can help customers get unavailable sizes or styles.

Given the product is unavailable in the current store, when the employee searches the network, then stock at other locations is displayed and the employee can start a reservation or transfer request.

To-be process

  1. Customer asks for a product.
  2. Staff check local inventory. If available, complete the sale.
  3. If not, search network-wide inventory.
  4. If stock exists elsewhere, reserve or transfer it. If not, check the warehouse and trigger replenishment.
  5. Record the interaction in the CRM for follow-up and future sales.

Odoo fit-gap evaluation

I evaluated Odoo against each requirement to separate what standard configuration covers from what needs a process or system change. Modules considered: Inventory (a location per store, stock movement tracking), Reordering rules (thresholds and demand-based replenishment), CRM (customer records, pipeline, follow-up activities), Purchase (order and approval workflow) and Stock transfers between locations.

Non-functional requirements: fast inventory display, at least 99.5% availability in operating hours, restricted access to customer data, usability after basic onboarding, and consistent data across stores.

Expected impact: better inventory visibility, demand-driven allocation, cross-store transfers, stronger CRM follow-up and less reliance on manual processes.

Property Rental Management System

Business analysis and process modelling

Business problem

The rental operation relied on spreadsheets with duplicated information. Keeping the data accurate was hard, and maintenance requests were handled inefficiently.

My approach

  1. Process analysis: identified operational issues and inefficient maintenance workflows.
  2. Process modelling: modelled the business processes and system interactions in Microsoft Visio, Lucidchart and Miro.
  3. Data modelling: built an ER model and applied normalisation from 1NF to 3NF to keep data consistent.
  4. Requirements and design: defined system requirements, business rules and database constraints for a standardised rental management system.

Takeaway: business process, then requirements, then system model, then data model. Each step feeds the next.

Victorian Property Sales

Data analysis and visualisation in Power BI

Business problem

Which regions and property types offer which price points? I analysed Victorian property sales to find pricing and sales trends that could support property decisions for different customer segments.

The data

The Victorian Property Sales Report (yearly summary) covers three areas: Country Victoria, the Melbourne Metropolitan Area and Victoria overall. Over a 15-month period it includes property types, number of sales, median prices and percentage change. It had six features, and several irrelevant columns needed to be removed before analysis.

Approach

  1. Select the features that matter, keeping median price as essential.
  2. Clean the data: remove errors, inconsistencies and duplicates, and turn raw rows into a usable format.
  3. Compare trends across regions and property types.
  4. Build interactive Power BI visuals of prices and sales.

Insight: for customers looking for a lower price segment, a unit or apartment in Country Victoria stood out, with a median price of only $420,000 AUD compared with the other segments.

Data source: Victorian Property Sales Report
